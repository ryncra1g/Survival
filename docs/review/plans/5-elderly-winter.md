**[Good to know]**

- **If** A CO alarm sounds or anyone has headache, dizziness or nausea → Everyone outside to fresh air now. Call 911. Don't go back in until responders say it's safe.
- **If** Power is out more than 4 hours → Discard refrigerated perishables that rose above 40°F. Keep the freezer closed (a full freezer holds about 48 hours).
- **If** A boil-water notice is issued or tap water looks/smells wrong → Use stored water; disinfect any other water (see Water).
- **If** Indoor temperature drops below 55°F → Move everyone into the warm room; close off the rest of the house.
- **If** Indoor temperature drops below 45°F with no safe heat source → Go to a warming center, shelter, or friends/family with power.
- **If** Outdoor temperature forecast below 20°F during an outage → Start the pipe-freeze protocol (see Heat & warmth).
- **If** Officials issue an evacuation order for your zone → Leave. Take go-bags and pets to the shelter or destination officials name.
- **If** Sewage backs up or toilets can't flush → Stop flushing; switch to the two-bucket toilet (see Sanitation).

- **If** Tap water is still running and no advisory → Use tap water first; save stored water.
- **If** Boil-water notice → Boil or disinfect tap water, or use stored water.
- **If** No tap water → Use stored water at 1 gal/day; use bathtub water for flushing.

> **INFO: How to disinfect water**
> If water is cloudy, let it settle or filter it first. Then disinfect it with regular unscented bleach, using the amount and wait time on the bleach label and in EPA's emergency disinfection steps, or bring it to a rolling boil for 1 minute (3 minutes above 6,500 ft), as CDC advises. Never guess an amount.

**Safe water, in this order**

| Way | When | What it does not remove |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Stored water | Drink it first. | Nothing to treat if it was stored clean; replace tap-filled water every 6 months. |
| 2. Household filter (gravity or pump) | The main way to make more water. | Viruses and chemicals: disinfect after filtering. |
| 3. Treatment tablets | A light backup to the filter. | Chemicals and fuel. Iodine and chlorine tablets don't reliably kill Cryptosporidium; chlorine dioxide can, when you follow the label. |
| 4. Boiling | The fallback if you have no filter or tablets. | Chemicals, fuel and toxins. |
| 5. Straw or bottle filter | In your go-bag, for one person. | Viruses and chemicals. |

> **WARNING: Fuel or chemicals in the water**
> Water with fuel or toxic chemicals in it can't be made safe by boiling or disinfecting. Use stored or bottled water, and contact your health department.

This plan never gives treatment amounts: follow the product label and CDC/EPA guidance.

### 9. Cooking & refrigeration

_Plan how you'll cook without power and when to throw out fridge food. The four-hour rule keeps everyone from getting sick._

**[Good to know]**

A gas range usually works in an outage if you light the burners with a match (check your manual; ovens with electronic controls usually won't work). Run the range hood or crack a window, and keep the CO alarm working.

- **If** Fridge above 40°F for more than 4 hours → Throw out meat, dairy, leftovers and cut produce. When in doubt, throw it out.
- **If** Freezer food still has ice crystals or is at 40°F or below → Safe to refreeze or cook.
- **If** Cooler use → Keep it in the shade and packed with ice; check with a thermometer.

> **DANGER: Cook outdoors only**
> Grills, camp stoves and charcoal give off carbon monoxide. Use them outdoors only — never inside, in a garage or on an enclosed porch.

### 6. Heat & warmth

_Winter is the most dangerous part of an outage in the Northern Minnesota area. Your plan uses a layered approach: keep the main heat running safely if you can, then shrink to one warm room, then relocate if it gets too cold._

**[Good to know]**

> **WARNING: Backup heater rules**
> Use only a propane heater labeled for indoor use with an oxygen depletion sensor (ODS) and tip-over shutoff, follow its manual's ventilation requirement, keep 3 feet of clearance, never leave it running while everyone sleeps, and keep a working CO alarm in the same room.

- One warm room: pick a small interior room with few windows (not the basement if it floods). Everyone sleeps there.
- Close doors to unused rooms; hang blankets over windows and doorways; put rugs/towels at door gaps.
- Layer up: base layer, fleece, hat, socks; cold-rated sleeping bags at night.
- Eat and drink warm calories; keep kids and older adults warm first.

**Pipe-freeze protocol**

| When | Do this |
| --- | --- |
| Before winter | Insulate exposed pipes; cover outdoor faucets; know where the main shutoff is. |
| Outage + below 20°F outside | Open cabinet doors under sinks; let faucets on outside walls drip. |
| House below 40°F for 12+ hours | Shut off the main water valve and open all faucets to drain lines (lowest first). |
| Pipe bursts | Shut off main water; call a plumber; move valuables; photograph damage for insurance. |

- **If** Furnace out and house above 55°F → Close up the house, layer up, wait.
- **If** House below 55°F → Move to the warm room.
- **If** Warm room still too cold → Use the indoor-rated propane heater in that room with the CO alarm, following its manual.
- **If** House below 45°F and dropping → Relocate to a warming center, friends/family, or your fallback location.

> **DANGER: Carbon monoxide**
> Never run a generator, grill, camp stove, charcoal or any fuel-burning device not rated for indoor use inside your home, garage, basement, crawlspace, tent or on a balcony — not even with doors or windows open. Keep generators outdoors at least 20 feet from doors, windows and vents, with the exhaust pointed away from buildings. Put battery-powered or battery-backup CO alarms on every level and outside sleeping areas. If a CO alarm sounds or anyone feels dizzy, headachy or nauseous, get everyone outside to fresh air right away and call 911.

### 7. Power & fuel math

_Plan on about 12 generator hours a day, burning 3–4.8 gallons of gasoline. Stored fuel of 10 gallons lasts about 2.1–3.3 days._

**[Good to know]**

**Fuel math at 12 hours/day (furnace/boiler blower + ignition in cold weather, plus fridge and charging)**

| Fuel | Per day | Runtime |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 5 gal gasoline | 3–4.8 gal | 1–1.7 days |
| 20 gal gasoline | 3–4.8 gal | 4.2–6.7 days |
| 40 gal gasoline | 3–4.8 gal | 8.3–13.3 days |
| One 20-lb propane tank | 4.2–7.2 gal | 0.7–1.1 days |
| One 100-lb propane cylinder | 4.2–7.2 gal | 3.3–5.6 days |

- Recommended stored gasoline for your scenario: 10 gal (2 5-gal cans) = 2.1–3.3 days of runtime.
- Stretch fuel: run in blocks (for example 2 hours on, 2 off) — a full fridge stays cold for hours between runs.
- Gasoline goes stale: add stabilizer and rotate into your cars every 12 months. Propane doesn't go stale, so it's better for long storage.

- **If** Power out, short outage expected → Use power banks/power station; keep fridge closed.
- **If** Utility lines down near home → Stay 35+ ft away and call the utility.

> **DANGER: Connecting a generator to your house**
> Never back-feed your house through a dryer outlet or any double-male ("suicide") cord — it can electrocute you, your neighbors and utility line workers, and start fires. Power house circuits only through a power inlet with an interlock or a transfer switch installed by a licensed electrician. Until then, plug appliances directly into the generator with outdoor-rated extension cords.

> **DANGER: Carbon monoxide**
> Never run a generator, grill, camp stove, charcoal or any fuel-burning device not rated for indoor use inside your home, garage, basement, crawlspace, tent or on a balcony — not even with doors or windows open. Keep generators outdoors at least 20 feet from doors, windows and vents, with the exhaust pointed away from buildings. Put battery-powered or battery-backup CO alarms on every level and outside sleeping areas. If a CO alarm sounds or anyone feels dizzy, headachy or nauseous, get everyone outside to fresh air right away and call 911.
