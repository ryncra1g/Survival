3. DAY 0 CHECKLIST & DECISION TRIGGERS
When an outage or storm is coming, run this checklist. Once it hits, use the if/then table so decisions are made in advance, not under stress.

[ ] Check CO and smoke alarms; replace batteries if unsure. (Carbon monoxide safety (CDC): https://www.cdc.gov/carbon-monoxide/about/index.html)
[ ] Charge every phone, power bank and power station. (Power outages (Ready.gov): https://www.ready.gov/power-outages)
[ ] Fill the bathtub water bladder and spare containers. (Make water safe to drink (EPA): https://www.epa.gov/ground-water-and-drinking-water/emergency-disinfection-drinking-water)
[ ] Set fridge to its coldest setting; fill empty freezer space with water bottles. (Food safety in a power outage (FoodSafety.gov): https://www.foodsafety.gov/food-safety-charts/food-safety-during-power-outage)
[ ] Get cash and refill prescriptions if you can. (Older adults and people with disabilities (Ready.gov): https://www.ready.gov/older-adults)
[ ] Fuel or charge vehicles. (Plan your evacuation (Ready.gov): https://www.ready.gov/evacuation)
[ ] Test-start the generator outdoors; stage fuel and cords. (Carbon monoxide safety (CDC): https://www.cdc.gov/carbon-monoxide/about/index.html)
[ ] Set up your warm room before the cold arrives (see Heat & warmth). (Winter weather (Ready.gov): https://www.ready.gov/winter-weather)
[ ] Bring pets inside and stage their food, meds and carrier. (Prepare your pets (Ready.gov): https://www.ready.gov/pets)
[ ] Check on elderly or disabled neighbors. (Older adults and people with disabilities (Ready.gov): https://www.ready.gov/older-adults)
IF A CO alarm sounds or anyone has headache, dizziness or nausea -> Everyone outside to fresh air now. Call 911. Don't go back in until responders say it's safe.
IF Power is out more than 4 hours -> Discard refrigerated perishables that rose above 40°F. Keep the freezer closed (a full freezer holds about 48 hours).
IF A boil-water notice is issued or tap water looks/smells wrong -> Use stored water; disinfect any other water (see Water).
IF Indoor temperature drops below 55°F -> Move everyone into the warm room; close off the rest of the house.
IF Indoor temperature drops below 45°F with no safe heat source -> Go to your fallback location or a warming center.
IF Outdoor temperature forecast below 20°F during an outage -> Start the pipe-freeze protocol (see Heat & warmth).
IF Officials issue an evacuation order for your zone -> Leave. Take go-bags and pets to your fallback location.
IF Sewage backs up or toilets can't flush -> Stop flushing; switch to the two-bucket toilet (see Sanitation).
IF A hurricane watch is issued -> Do the Day 0 checklist; put up window protection; decide stay vs. go before the warning.

NOTE: How to disinfect water: If water is cloudy, let it settle or filter it first. Then disinfect it one of two ways. Use regular unscented bleach, with the amount and wait time on the bleach label and in EPA's emergency disinfection steps. Or bring it to a rolling boil for 1 minute (3 minutes above 6,500 ft), as CDC advises. Never guess an amount.
Safe water, in this order
Way | When | What it does not remove
1. Stored water | Drink it first. | Nothing to treat if it was stored clean; replace tap-filled water every 6 months.
2. Household filter (gravity or pump) | The main way to make more water. | Viruses and chemicals: disinfect after filtering.
3. Treatment tablets | A light backup to the filter. | Chemicals and fuel. Iodine and chlorine tablets don't reliably kill Cryptosporidium; chlorine dioxide can, when you follow the label.
4. Boiling | The fallback if you have no filter or tablets. | Chemicals, fuel and toxins.
5. Straw or bottle filter | In your go-bag, for one person. | Viruses and chemicals.
NOTE: Fuel or chemicals in the water: Water with fuel or toxic chemicals in it can't be made safe by boiling or disinfecting. Use stored or bottled water, and contact your health department.

9. COOKING & REFRIGERATION
Plan how you'll cook without power and when to throw out fridge food. The four-hour rule keeps everyone from getting sick.

A gas range usually works in an outage if you light the burners with a match (check your manual; ovens with electronic controls usually won't work). Run the range hood or crack a window, and keep the CO alarm working.
IF Fridge above 40°F for more than 4 hours -> Throw out meat, dairy, leftovers and cut produce. When in doubt, throw it out.
IF Freezer food still has ice crystals or is at 40°F or below -> Safe to refreeze or cook.
IF Running a generator -> Run fridge/freezer 1–2 hours at a time; keep doors shut between runs.
IF Cooler use -> Keep it in the shade and packed with ice; check with a thermometer.
SAFETY: Cook outdoors only: Grills, camp stoves and charcoal give off carbon monoxide. Use them outdoors only — never inside, in a garage or on an enclosed porch.
Carbon monoxide safety (CDC): https://www.cdc.gov/carbon-monoxide/about/index.html

== POWER & HEAT ==

6. HEAT & WARMTH
Winter is the most dangerous part of an outage in the Long Island area. Your plan uses a layered approach: keep the main heat running safely if you can, then shrink to one warm room, then relocate if it gets too cold.

Your natural gas heat needs electricity for the blower, ignition or circulator pump. The safest way to keep it running is a generator (recommended below) connected through a licensed electrician's power inlet and interlock (or a transfer switch). Furnaces draw roughly 400–800 W running, so a 2,500 W+ inverter generator can handle it along with the fridge.
NOTE: Backup heater rules: Use only a propane heater labeled for indoor use. It must have an oxygen depletion sensor (ODS) and a tip-over shutoff. Follow its manual's ventilation requirement and keep 3 feet of clearance. Never leave it running while you sleep. Keep a working CO alarm in the same room.
- One warm room: pick a small interior room with few windows (not the basement if it floods). Everyone sleeps there.
- Close doors to unused rooms; hang blankets over windows and doorways; put rugs/towels at door gaps.
- Layer up: base layer, fleece, hat, socks; cold-rated sleeping bags at night.
- Eat and drink warm calories; keep kids warm first.
Pipe-freeze protocol
When | Do this
Before winter | Insulate exposed pipes; cover outdoor faucets; know where the main shutoff is.
Outage + below 20°F outside | Open cabinet doors under sinks; let faucets on outside walls drip.
House below 40°F for 12+ hours | Shut off the main water valve and open all faucets to drain lines (lowest first).
Pipe bursts | Shut off main water; call a plumber; move valuables; photograph damage for insurance.
IF Furnace out and house above 55°F -> Close up the house, layer up, wait.
IF House below 55°F -> Run the furnace from the generator via the inlet/interlock; or move to the warm room.
IF Warm room still too cold -> Use the indoor-rated propane heater in that room with the CO alarm, following its manual.
IF House below 45°F and dropping -> Relocate to a warming center, friends/family, or your fallback location.
SAFETY: Carbon monoxide: Never run a generator, grill, camp stove, charcoal or any fuel-burning device not rated for indoor use inside your home, garage, basement, crawlspace, tent or on a balcony — not even with doors or windows open. Keep generators outdoors at least 20 feet from doors, windows and vents, with the exhaust pointed away from buildings. Put battery-powered or battery-backup CO alarms on every level and outside sleeping areas. If a CO alarm sounds or anyone feels dizzy, headachy or nauseous, get everyone outside to fresh air right away and call 911.

7. POWER & FUEL MATH
Plan on about 12 generator hours a day, burning 3–4.8 gallons of gasoline. Stored fuel of 30 gallons lasts about 6.3–10 days.

You don't have backup power yet. A dual-fuel inverter generator is the best value for your home; a battery power station covers quiet overnight loads.

- Recommended stored gasoline for your scenario: 30 gal (6 5-gal cans) = 6.3–10 days of runtime.
- Stretch fuel: run in blocks (for example 2 hours on, 2 off) — a full fridge stays cold for hours between runs.
- Gasoline goes stale: add stabilizer and rotate into your cars every 12 months. Propane doesn't go stale, so it's better for long storage.
- For a long outage you can't store enough gasoline: plan on propane cylinders, rationed runtime, and refueling when stations reopen.
IF Power out, short outage expected -> Use power banks/power station; keep fridge closed.
IF Power out more than 4 hours -> Set the generator outdoors 20+ ft from the house, exhaust away; run fridge/freezer cycles.
IF Generator needs refueling -> Shut it off and let it cool for 15+ minutes before refueling.
IF Rain or snow -> Use a generator tent/cover rated for it, still in open air — never move it into the garage.
IF Utility lines down near home -> Stay 35+ ft away and call the utility.
SAFETY: Connecting a generator to your house: Never back-feed your house through a dryer outlet or any double-male ("suicide") cord — it can electrocute you, your neighbors and utility line workers, and start fires. Until then, plug appliances directly into the generator with outdoor-rated extension cords.

Keep a low profile and build a small neighbor pod. Neighbors who look out for each other are the best security in a long outage.

- Lock doors and windows; keep exterior lights on a battery or solar motion light.
- Neighbor pod: swap numbers with 3–5 nearby households, share skills (medical, tools, childcare), and check on older neighbors.
- If you feel unsafe, leave and call 911. Supplies can be replaced; people can't.
